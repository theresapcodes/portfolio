The problem

Your product is starting to look like it was built by five different people.

Most early-stage teams never set out to build an inconsistent product. It happens one screen at a time. A button gets rebuilt in three places. Three shades of blue show up. Engineers ask which version is right, and nobody wants to be the one to say.

The cost arrives later, as slower releases, longer reviews, and a product that feels a little different on every page. A small, well-built system fixes that early, while it is still cheap to do.

How it works

Three weeks, one clear scope, and a midweek check-in so nothing surprises you.

  1. Week 1: Audit and alignI review your product and brand, then meet with your design and engineering leads. We agree on what the system covers and what it leaves out.
  2. Week 2: BuildTokens come first, then components, with a review session midweek. I use AI tools to speed up first drafts and variations. I review every output by hand before it goes into the system.
  3. Week 3: Document and hand offI write the usage guidance, walk your team through it, and leave you a clear list of what to build next.

Works with shadcn/ui

Many startup teams build with shadcn/ui, and I like it. It gives your engineers well-made components they can edit, which is a strong place to start.

What it does not give you is the layer around it. Your brand decisions, a Figma library your designers can work in, and written rules for how everything fits still need someone to make them. That is the work I do, and I build it to sit on top of what your engineers already use.

  • Your brand, mapped in. Your colors, type, spacing, and corner radius go into shadcn's theme variables, so the components your engineers already use look like your product, not the default theme.
  • A Figma library that matches. Your designers get components built on the same tokens, so what they design is what gets built.
  • Rules everyone can find. Spec pages that say when to use each component, how it behaves, and what it needs to be accessible.
  • One source of truth. A single token file that design and code both follow, so changing a brand color means changing one value.

On a different stack? The tokens and documentation carry over to other frameworks and native apps.

Who it's for

This works best for startups with a live product and a small team building it.

A good fit if

  • You are a seed to Series B company with a product in market.
  • Your team is founder-led, with no dedicated design leader.
  • Your product has started to feel inconsistent from screen to screen.
  • Engineers and designers are rebuilding the same things.

Probably not yet if

  • You are still testing ideas and have no product to systemize.
  • You need governance across several brands or a large organization. That is a longer engagement, and we can scope it separately.

FAQ

Will you work with our engineers?
Yes. I meet with your engineering lead in week 1 and run a handoff session in week 3. You also get a token file set up to use in code.
Why not just use shadcn/ui?
You can, and if your team already uses it, I build on top of it. It is a strong set of components for React and Tailwind. What it leaves open is what makes a product feel like yours: your brand decisions in one place, and a Figma library plus written rules that keep designers and engineers working from the same page. Without those, teams end up with the default look and a little more drift with each release. The Design System Starter is that missing layer.
Do you use AI?
Yes, for first drafts, variations, and documentation. I review everything and make the design decisions myself.
What do you need from us?
One point person and access to your product and design files.
